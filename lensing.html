What am I seeing?

Massive objects bend the path of light passing near them — a prediction of Einstein’s general relativity called gravitational lensing. This demo places a simulated lens (like a galaxy or dark-matter halo) in front of the Hubble Ultra Deep Field and ray-traces every pixel in real time.

The controls

  • Einstein radius sets the lens mass. Sources directly behind the lens are smeared into rings and arcs at this radius.
  • Ellipticity flattens the lens (like a real elliptical galaxy), splitting the ring into multiple arcs. The yellow curve in the preview is the caustic — sources inside it are multiply imaged.
  • Position angle rotates the flattened lens.
  • The Galaxy Cluster preset sums the deflection from several member galaxies at fixed relative positions — drag to move the whole cluster, and Einstein radius scales its total mass. Ellipticity/position angle are fixed per member in this mode.

Interactions

  • Move the mouse (or drag a finger) to place the lens; scroll or pinch to change its mass.
  • Space or click / two-finger tap — lock & unlock the lens
  • ←→↑↓ — nudge the lens · +/− — change mass
  • A orbit · R reset · H hide controls · I this info
